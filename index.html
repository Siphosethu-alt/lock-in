September 2026 · Age 21

The
Lock-In.

I wrote this before knowing exactly where the next opportunity would take me. These aren't rules for becoming obsessed with work. They're reminders of how I want to live when the next chapter begins.

Read the rules ↓
01

Get out of the room.

You've already spent enough time inside waiting for life to start moving again. When the opportunity comes, don't replace one kind of isolation with another. Study at the library. Sit around other people. Work outside sometimes. Walk around. Learn the place you're living in. Be there.

02

Stop living from the bed.

Wake up and leave it. You know how easily an hour in bed becomes three. You don't need some ridiculous 5 AM routine, but you do need mornings again. Get up early, get ready and start the day. Weekends and freezing mornings can have some mercy.

03

Learn how to learn anywhere.

Stop needing the perfect room, perfect silence, perfect mood or perfect amount of motivation. Sit around people and concentrate anyway. That's a skill too.

04

Learn alone. Practise with people.

You should be able to take something you don't understand, sit with it and eventually figure it out. But don't make learning lonely. Find people who are serious. Compare approaches. Build together. Explain things. Ask questions. Let somebody challenge you.

05

Don't wait for people to find you.

Go say hello. Join the table. Ask what people are doing after class. Accept the invitation sometimes. Invite somebody yourself sometimes. You already know what happens when you stay in your own space too much. Make yourself part of the place.

06

Touch grass.

Actually. There should be days where the laptop closes and you just go somewhere. Walk. Explore. Meet someone at the mall. Take pictures. Have stories that have absolutely nothing to do with cybersecurity, software or whatever you're studying.

07

Look after the body carrying you through this.

Exercise. Walk. Sleep properly. Eat properly when you can. You want your mind working at full capacity for months, not two spectacular weeks followed by exhaustion.

08

Don't turn discipline into punishment.

You're still allowed girlfriends, friends, music, games, stupid conversations, weekends and doing absolutely nothing sometimes. The objective isn't to become miserable enough to prove that you're serious.

09

Take pictures of the ordinary days.

Not just certificates and achievements. Your first room. The route to class. Your desk. People you become close with. Random Saturdays. Yourself at 21. You're going to forget what this period actually felt like otherwise.

10

Keep showing up.

You don't need to become a different person every Monday. You don't need another dramatic restart every time you slip. A bad Tuesday is just a bad Tuesday. Come back Wednesday.

The point

The Lock-In isn't about locking yourself in a room.

It's a year of actually showing up for your own life.
